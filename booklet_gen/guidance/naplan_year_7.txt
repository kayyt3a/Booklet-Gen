YEAR 7 SUPPLEMENT

This supplement narrows the general guide to Year 7 expectations. Where it
disagrees with a general statement, follow this supplement. Year 7 is the
first secondary year, so algebra becomes genuine rather than arithmetic with a
letter attached, and reading questions begin to ask how a text works and not
only what it says.

NUMERACY AT YEAR 7

Work mostly within these expectations:

- Integers, including all four operations and the number line.
- Index notation for whole numbers, square numbers and square roots, and
  prime factorisation.
- Fractions, decimals and percentages, converting between them, and finding a
  percentage of a quantity.
- Ratio, rate and simple proportion in everyday contexts.
- Algebraic conventions, substitution, collecting like terms, expanding a
  single bracket, and solving one and two step linear equations.
- Plotting and interpreting a linear relationship from a rule or a table.
- Area of triangles, parallelograms and trapeziums, circumference introduced
  informally, and volume and surface area of rectangular prisms.
- Angle relationships at a point, on a line, in a triangle and in a
  quadrilateral, and angles formed by a transversal crossing parallel lines.
- Transformations on the Cartesian plane in all four quadrants.
- Mean, median, mode and range, dot plots, stem and leaf plots and column
  graphs, and describing what a measure of centre does and does not tell you.
- Probability of a single event as a fraction, decimal or percentage, and
  listing a sample space.

Some arithmetic should be completable without a calculator. Do not assume
calculator access unless the question says so.

Stay away from: quadratic expressions, trigonometry, index laws with variable
exponents, and formal simultaneous equations.

READING AT YEAR 7

Passages of roughly 300 to 500 words, including at least one text where the
writer's stance matters. Suitable forms include feature articles, opinion
pieces, short fiction with a clear narrative voice, and explanatory texts.

Ask about implied meaning, the writer's purpose and position, tone, the effect
of a specific language or structural choice, how evidence supports a claim,
and the difference between a fact and an interpretation. Comparison across two
short texts is appropriate.

CONVENTIONS AT YEAR 7

- Clause types and how subordination changes emphasis.
- Active and passive voice, and why a writer might choose either.
- Modality and its effect on how strongly a claim reads.
- Semicolons, colons, dashes used sparingly, and punctuation that resolves
  ambiguity.
- Cohesion across paragraphs: reference, substitution and connectives.
- Less common homophones, and morphology and word origin as spelling tools.

WRITING AT YEAR 7

Prompts should support a sustained response with a controlled structure and a
clear sense of audience. A narrative should allow tension and a resolution
that is earned. A persuasive prompt should support a defensible position with
developed reasoning and acknowledgement of an alternative view. Short items may
practise thesis statements, paragraph structure, evidence integration and
editing for concision.
