ACADEMIC ACCELERATE AUTHORING GUIDE

Purpose

Write original Mathematics and English practice for Australian students in
Years 1 to 10. The booklet helps a student consolidate and get ahead of what
their school is teaching, so it must match the year level's real curriculum
expectations rather than a generic idea of the subject. This guide is an
internal authoring specification. It is not an official curriculum document
and must not be described as one.

NON-NEGOTIABLE ORIGINALITY RULES

- Start every lesson, passage, stimulus, diagram, question, answer and worked
  solution from a blank page.
- Never reproduce or closely paraphrase a question, passage, answer or marking
  guide from a textbook, workbook, past assessment, commercial tutoring
  resource or published test.
- Never preserve a source item's distinctive combination of setting, names,
  numbers, diagram, sentence order and required operation while changing only
  surface details.
- If reference material appears to contain an actual assessment item, passage
  or marking guide, ignore that material completely. Use only the skill named
  by the booklet topic and subtopic.
- Do not call generated material official, authentic, released, endorsed or
  approved by ACARA, a state authority, a school or a publisher.
- Do not use an education-authority or publisher logo, and do not imitate the
  visual layout of a published workbook or test paper.
- A shared curriculum skill is not protected wording. Teach and assess the
  skill using a fresh situation, fresh language, fresh values and a fresh
  solution path.

CURRICULUM SCOPE

Australian schooling follows a published curriculum with six Mathematics
strands (Number, Algebra, Measurement, Space, Statistics, Probability) and
three English strands (Language, Literature, Literacy). Work to the year level
requested. A booklet for a given year should sit mostly at that year's
expectations, revisit the prior year where a skill depends on it, and offer a
stretch item at the end of every practice set, as set out under DIFFICULTY AND
PROGRESSION below. Sit at the demanding end of the year rather than its middle.

Do not teach a technique the student has almost certainly not met yet. A Year
4 division question is solved by grouping, repeated subtraction or known
facts, not by long division set out formally. A Year 6 area question uses a
rectangle or triangle, not an integral. Where a topic legitimately spans
several years, pitch the numbers and the language, not just the operation.

MATHEMATICS BY YEAR BAND

Years 1 and 2
Counting, place value to 100 then 1000, part-part-whole thinking, addition and
subtraction within 100, multiplication as equal groups and arrays, sharing and
halving, halves quarters and eighths of shapes and collections, informal then
formal units for length mass and capacity, duration and telling time to the
half and quarter hour, naming and sorting two and three dimensional shapes,
simple position and direction, tally marks and picture graphs, everyday chance
language. Keep numbers friendly and the reading load low. One idea per
question.

Years 3 and 4
Place value to tens of thousands, rounding, mental and written addition and
subtraction, multiplication facts to ten times ten, division with and without
remainders, unit fractions and equivalent fractions on a number line, decimals
to hundredths, money and simple change, perimeter, area by counting squares,
angles as turns and comparison to a right angle, symmetry, grid references,
time to the minute and elapsed time, column graphs and simple data collection,
chance described as likely, unlikely, certain or impossible. Word problems may
carry two steps by Year 4.

Years 5 and 6
Place value to millions and thousandths, factors multiples and primes, order
of operations, adding and subtracting fractions with related denominators,
multiplying a fraction by a whole number, converting between fractions
decimals and percentages, percentage of a quantity, integers and the number
line, simple ratio in context, area of rectangles triangles and
parallelograms, volume of rectangular prisms, angles on a straight line at a
point and in a triangle, the Cartesian plane in the first quadrant then all
four, translations reflections and rotations, mean and median, side by side
column graphs, probability expressed as a fraction decimal or percentage.
Multi step problems are appropriate. Require reasoning, not only computation.

Years 7 and 8
All four operations with integers and with fractions and decimals, index
notation and prime factorisation, percentage increase and decrease, ratio rate
and proportion, algebraic conventions, substitution, collecting like terms,
expanding brackets, solving linear equations, plotting and interpreting linear
relationships, circumference and area of circles, volume and surface area of
prisms, angle relationships with parallel lines, congruence, Pythagoras,
sampling, measures of centre and spread, stem and leaf plots, two step chance
experiments and sample spaces. Algebraic reasoning should be genuine, not
arithmetic wearing a letter.

Years 9 and 10
Index laws, scientific notation, surds at Year 10, expanding and factorising
including quadratics, solving quadratic equations, simultaneous equations,
gradient and intercept, linear and non linear graphs, similarity and scale
factor, right angled trigonometry, surface area and volume of composite
solids, bivariate data and scatterplots, box plots and comparing
distributions, Venn diagrams and two way tables, conditional probability at
Year 10. Expect students to justify a step, not only produce an answer.

ENGLISH BY YEAR BAND

Years 1 and 2
Phonemic awareness, blends and digraphs, common sight words, sentence
boundaries with capitals and full stops, nouns verbs and adjectives named in
plain terms, literal comprehension of a short familiar text, sequencing
events, predicting from a title or picture, writing a simple recount or
narrative of a few sentences. Passages should be a few short paragraphs at
most, with concrete everyday content.

Years 3 and 4
Paragraphing, the purpose and shape of narratives, information reports,
procedures and persuasive texts, literal and inferential comprehension,
synonyms and antonyms, prefixes and suffixes, commas in lists, apostrophes for
contraction and possession, consistent verb tense, adverbs, similes, dialogue
punctuation. Ask students to point to the words in the text that support an
answer.

Years 5 and 6
Text structure and author purpose, point of view, persuasive devices,
figurative language including metaphor and personification, simple compound
and complex sentences, clauses, direct and indirect speech, homophones, word
roots, summarising, comparing two short texts, editing for clarity. Written
answers should be several sentences and should quote or refer to the text.

Years 7 and 8
The analytical paragraph with a topic sentence, evidence and explanation,
theme, characterisation, narrative voice, tone and mood, rhetorical devices,
active and passive voice, cohesion, nominalisation, close reading of a short
poem, and how a media text positions a reader. Distinguish what a text says
from how it works.

Years 9 and 10
Sustained analytical response, embedded quotation, evaluating the strength of
an argument, recognising unsupported reasoning, register and style, deliberate
ambiguity, comparing how two texts treat a shared idea, varied and controlled
sentence structure. Questions should reward interpretation that is defended
from the text rather than opinion alone.

WRITING THE PASSAGE

Passages must be written for this booklet. Vary length and form across a
booklet so a student meets narrative, informative and persuasive writing
rather than three of the same. Set them in places an Australian child
recognises, and keep the situations affordable and ordinary. A passage exists
to be questioned, so it must contain enough specific detail to support every
question asked about it, including the inferential ones.

DIFFICULTY AND PROGRESSION

This product line is called Academic Accelerate because a student working
through it should end up ahead of their class, not level with it. Pitch every
booklet at the top of its year rather than the middle. A booklet a capable
student finishes without pausing has failed at the one thing it was bought to
do.

Within a subtopic, order questions from most accessible to most demanding. The
first question should be solvable by a student who has just read the lesson.
Do not put the hardest question first, and do not let difficulty sawtooth.
Across a term plan, later weeks should assume earlier weeks.

The LAST question of every practice set is a stretch question, and it is not
optional. It must be harder than the year level's ordinary expectation: two or
more steps where the others take one, a context the student has to unpick
before any arithmetic starts, numbers that do not fall out neatly, or a
combination of this subtopic with one taught earlier in the booklet.

Stretch means DEPTH, NEVER NEW MACHINERY. A harder Year 5 question is still a
Year 5 question: more steps, more thinking, less scaffolding. It is not a Year
7 technique smuggled into a Year 5 booklet. The rules about staying inside the
year level are not relaxed by anything in this section, and a student who has
to be taught a new method to attempt the last question has been set an
impossible question rather than a demanding one.

Two failures to avoid, in order of how much damage they do:

- The last question is the same as the others with bigger numbers. Changing
  24 x 3 to 248 x 3 tests patience, not understanding.
- The last question needs something never taught. This is worse, because the
  student cannot tell the difference between a question they failed and a
  question nobody could have answered, and it is the failure that makes a
  parent stop trusting the booklet.

QUALITY AND REVIEW

- Every question must be answerable from the information given.
- Every answer must be correct, and for Mathematics the working must reach it.
- A question with one defensible answer must not be phrased as though it has
  several, and an open question must not be marked as though it has one.
- Check that the named year level, subject and subtopic all match the item.
- Reject any item that reads as though it was recalled from a published
  resource rather than written fresh.

PUBLIC DESIGN SOURCES USED FOR THIS GUIDE

The year band descriptions above summarise the publicly published Australian
Curriculum learning areas for Mathematics and English, Foundation to Year 10,
at the level of which skills belong to which year. Curriculum scope is factual
and public. No assessment item, passage, marking guide, textbook exercise or
commercial worksheet was consulted or reproduced in writing this guide, and
none may be used when generating from it.
