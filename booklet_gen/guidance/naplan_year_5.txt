YEAR 5 SUPPLEMENT

This supplement narrows the general guide to Year 5 expectations. Where it
disagrees with a general statement, follow this supplement. Year 5 students
can handle multi step problems and sustained passages, but they still benefit
from questions that name clearly what is being asked for.

NUMERACY AT YEAR 5

Work mostly within these expectations:

- Whole numbers beyond a million, place value, comparing, ordering and
  rounding.
- All four operations with whole numbers, including written multiplication by
  a two digit number and division with a remainder interpreted in context.
- Factors, multiples and prime numbers. Order of operations in simple cases.
- Equivalent fractions, comparing fractions, and adding and subtracting
  fractions with the same or related denominators.
- Decimals to thousandths, comparing and ordering, and multiplying or dividing
  by 10, 100 and 1000.
- Percentages as parts of a hundred, and the link between simple fractions,
  decimals and percentages.
- Perimeter and area of rectangles, and volume by counting cubic units.
- Twelve and twenty four hour time, timetables and elapsed time.
- Estimating and measuring angles with a protractor, and classifying angles.
- Translations, reflections, rotations, line and rotational symmetry, and the
  first quadrant of the Cartesian plane.
- Constructing and interpreting column graphs, line graphs and dot plots, and
  describing the shape of a small data set.
- Probability described with fractions, and listing outcomes of a single
  event.

Stay away from: algebraic equations with a pronumeral to solve, index
notation, negative numbers in operations, Pythagoras, and formal statistical
measures beyond a simple average.

READING AT YEAR 5

Passages of roughly 200 to 400 words. Vary narrative, informative and
persuasive writing, and include at least one text with a structural feature
worth asking about, such as a subheading, a sequence, or a contrast between
two viewpoints.

Ask for inference supported by evidence, author purpose and audience, the
effect of a word choice, the meaning of vocabulary from context, and how a
paragraph contributes to the whole. Comparison questions are appropriate when
two short texts are supplied.

CONVENTIONS AT YEAR 5

- Simple, compound and complex sentences, and identifying clauses.
- Commas for clauses and to prevent misreading.
- Apostrophes for singular and plural possession, distinguished from plurals.
- Punctuating direct speech.
- Homophones and commonly confused pairs.
- Prefixes, suffixes and word roots, including changes to the base word.
- Pronoun reference and consistent tense across a paragraph.
- Cohesion: connectives, paragraph order and topic sentences.

WRITING AT YEAR 5

Prompts should support a sustained response with paragraphing. A narrative
should allow character motivation and a resolution that follows from the
problem. A persuasive prompt should support a position, developed reasons with
elaboration, and a conclusion. Short items may practise planning, paragraph
order, sentence combining and editing.
