YEAR 3 SUPPLEMENT

This supplement narrows the general guide to Year 3 expectations. Where it
disagrees with a general statement, follow this supplement. Year 3 is the
first year a student sits NAPLAN, so keep instructions short, literal and
unambiguous, and keep the reading load of a numeracy question low enough that
it tests the mathematics rather than the reading.

NUMERACY AT YEAR 3

Work mostly within these expectations:

- Whole numbers to 10 000, place value, comparing and ordering, rounding to
  the nearest ten or hundred.
- Addition and subtraction with regrouping, using mental strategies and
  simple written methods.
- Multiplication facts for 2, 3, 5 and 10, arrays and equal groups. Division
  understood as sharing and as the inverse of multiplication.
- Unit fractions and simple fractions of a shape or collection: halves,
  thirds, quarters, fifths and eighths. Compare fractions with the same
  denominator.
- Money to the nearest five cents, totals and change.
- Length, mass and capacity in formal units, choosing a sensible unit.
- Telling time to the minute, days, months and calendar reading, and simple
  durations.
- Naming and describing two and three dimensional shapes by their features,
  symmetry, and following or giving directions on a simple grid.
- Collecting data, tally marks, and reading picture graphs and column graphs
  where one symbol or square represents one item.
- Chance described as certain, likely, unlikely or impossible.

Stay away from: decimals, percentages, formal long multiplication or division,
area formulas, angle measurement in degrees, and negative numbers.

Numbers should stay friendly. A two step problem is reasonable by the end of
Year 3, but each step should be short and the wording plain.

READING AT YEAR 3

Passages of roughly 100 to 250 words. Complete short narratives, simple
information texts, and straightforward persuasive pieces about familiar
things: pets, school, weather, sport, family outings, local animals.

Weight questions towards locating stated detail and sequencing, with some
straightforward inference where the evidence is close by and unambiguous.
Vocabulary questions should ask the meaning of a word the surrounding sentence
genuinely explains. Avoid subtle irony, unreliable narration and abstract
theme.

CONVENTIONS AT YEAR 3

- Capital letters, full stops and question marks.
- Commas in a list.
- Apostrophes for common contractions.
- Regular and common irregular plurals.
- Common homophones such as there, their, they're and to, too, two.
- Subject and verb agreement, and consistent simple tense.
- Naming nouns, verbs and adjectives in plain terms.
- Common prefixes and suffixes, and alphabetical order.

Spelling should draw on common letter patterns and sound knowledge rather than
on unusual words a Year 3 student would not have met.

WRITING AT YEAR 3

A narrative prompt should invite an orientation, a small problem and an ending.
A persuasive prompt should invite a clear opinion with two or three reasons
drawn from a child's own experience. Keep the stimulus concrete. Do not
require any specialist knowledge.
