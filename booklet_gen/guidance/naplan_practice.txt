COPYRIGHT-SAFE NAPLAN PRACTICE AUTHORING GUIDE

Purpose

Write original literacy and numeracy practice for Australian students. Match
the broad capabilities, clarity and range of difficulty associated with
NAPLAN preparation. Do not reproduce, reconstruct or disguise official test
content. This guide is an internal authoring specification, not an official
NAPLAN document.

NON-NEGOTIABLE ORIGINALITY RULES

- Start every lesson, passage, stimulus, diagram, question, writing prompt,
  answer and worked solution from a blank page.
- Never reproduce or closely paraphrase a past, current, demonstration or
  trial test question, answer, passage, writing stimulus or marking guide.
- Never preserve a source item's distinctive combination of setting, names,
  numbers, diagram, sentence order and required operation while merely
  changing surface details.
- If reference material appears to contain an actual assessment item, answer,
  passage, stimulus or marking guide, ignore that material completely. Use
  only the skill named by the booklet topic and subtopic.
- Do not predict a live writing prompt or claim that an item appeared, will
  appear, or is likely to appear in an official test.
- Do not call generated material official, authentic, released, endorsed or
  approved by ACARA or another assessment authority.
- Do not use an ACARA, NAPLAN, Australian Government or education-authority
  logo. Do not imitate the visual layout of an official test paper.
- A shared curriculum skill is not protected wording. Teach and assess the
  skill using a fresh situation, fresh language, fresh values and a fresh
  solution path.

ASSESSMENT SCOPE

NAPLAN is sat in Years 3, 5, 7 and 9. It covers four domains: reading,
writing, conventions of language, and numeracy. Questions should primarily
draw on skills expected from earlier schooling while allowing a small number
of accessible stretch items. Follow the requested subtopic and year level,
not memories of a particular paper.

FolioAI is printable, so adapt digital interaction types to paper honestly.
A drag-and-drop, hot-text or hotspot skill can become a short response,
matching, circling, labelling or evidence-selection task. Test the same
capability without claiming to reproduce the online interface.

GENERAL ITEM-WRITING CONTRACT

- Give each item one main purpose. Do not make difficult reading the hidden
  barrier in a numeracy item unless interpreting language is the intended
  skill.
- Include every fact needed to solve the item. Do not depend on specialist
  knowledge, paid experiences, brand knowledge or a particular household.
- Make the expected response clear. Avoid trick wording, double negatives and
  plausible alternative answers unless evaluating alternatives is the skill.
- Ensure the supplied answer and working fully resolve the exact question.
- Order a set from the plainest form of the skill to application, reasoning
  and transfer. Include a genuine range of difficulty without trying to make
  a child fail.
- Use concise Standard Australian English, Australian spelling, metric units,
  Australian dollars and unambiguous dates.
- Use invented people and institutions. Real geography can be used when it is
  accurate and culturally appropriate.
- Do not describe a student's result as pass or fail. NAPLAN is one source of
  information about learning, not a complete judgement of a child or school.

NUMERACY

Cover appropriate skills from number, algebra, measurement, space, statistics
and probability. Mix fluency with application, problem-solving and reasoning.

- Begin with a direct calculation or interpretation that reveals the target
  skill cleanly.
- Progress to one-step and multi-step applications using fresh everyday
  situations such as timetables, maps, sport, gardens, recipes, transport,
  collections, weather observations or community events.
- Ask students to interpret tables, graphs, scales, diagrams and spatial
  information, not merely calculate from prose.
- Use realistic values and units. State rounding expectations and whether an
  exact value is required.
- For Years 7 and 9, include some arithmetic that can be completed without a
  calculator. Do not make calculator access necessary unless the question
  explicitly says it is allowed.
- Create every diagram from the new question. A diagram must agree with the
  labels, values, answer and working.
- Wrong answer options are not available in the FolioAI booklet schema. Use a
  short response or ask the student to identify and correct an invented
  student's error instead.

READING

Use wholly original, complete texts. Across a booklet, vary informative,
imaginative and persuasive writing. A passage should make sense without
outside material and should finish rather than stopping like an extracted
page.

Across the questions attached to a passage, sample several capabilities:

- locate an explicitly stated detail
- connect details across sentences or paragraphs
- make an inference supported by textual evidence
- determine a word or phrase meaning from context
- identify purpose, audience, viewpoint or text structure
- explain the effect of a language choice
- distinguish fact from opinion where appropriate
- compare information or viewpoints when two short texts are supplied

Every inference must have evidence in the passage. The model answer should
name that evidence. Do not require one cultural interpretation where the text
reasonably supports several.

CONVENTIONS OF LANGUAGE

Assess spelling first as a skill in meaningful context, then grammar and
punctuation in Standard Australian English.

- Use original sentences and short paragraphs.
- Test one clear convention at a time before combining conventions.
- For spelling, use age-appropriate sound, letter-pattern, morphology and
  word-origin knowledge. Do not rely only on spotting a visually odd word.
- For grammar, include word classes, agreement, tense, clauses, sentence
  structure, reference and cohesion at the requested level.
- For punctuation, make meaning matter. Ask what a mark changes or where it
  clarifies the sentence, not only what the mark is called.
- Do not present a home dialect or a student's identity as defective. The
  task is to recognise and use Standard Australian English in the stated
  context.

WRITING

NAPLAN writing asks for a continuous narrative or persuasive response. A
FolioAI practice prompt must be newly invented and must not imitate the theme,
images or wording of a released or remembered prompt.

- A narrative prompt should support an orientation, a developing problem or
  tension, and a satisfying resolution without prescribing the whole plot.
- A persuasive prompt should support a clear position, developed reasons and
  a conclusion. It must not require specialist facts for a fair response.
- Use topics on which children can draw from imagination, observation and
  ordinary school or community life.
- Practise both narrative and persuasive writing over time. Never suggest
  that one form is predicted for the next test.
- Short booklet items can practise planning, paragraph order, cohesion,
  sentence craft, editing and audience awareness. Do not imply that a short
  response is equivalent to completing a full writing task.

QUALITY AND REVIEW

Before accepting an item, check that it is original, solvable, age-appropriate,
free of restricted source wording, and matched to the stated answer. Check
that names, settings and activities are varied without tokenism. An adult
should review generated material before it is given to a child.
