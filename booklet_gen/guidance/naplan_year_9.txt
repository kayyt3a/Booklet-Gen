YEAR 9 SUPPLEMENT

This supplement narrows the general guide to Year 9 expectations. Where it
disagrees with a general statement, follow this supplement. Year 9 students
should be asked to justify, evaluate and choose a method, not only to execute
one. Questions may reward a student who explains why an approach works.

NUMERACY AT YEAR 9

Work mostly within these expectations:

- Index laws with whole number exponents, scientific notation, and converting
  between recurring decimals and fractions in simple cases.
- Percentage increase and decrease, repeated change, rates, and direct
  proportion.
- Expanding binomial products and factorising simple expressions, including
  common factors and difference of two squares.
- Solving linear equations and inequalities, and simultaneous linear equations
  by substitution or graphing.
- Gradient, intercepts, the equation of a straight line, and recognising that
  a relationship is not linear from a table or graph.
- Pythagoras and right angled trigonometry, including finding an unknown side
  or angle in context.
- Similarity, scale factor, and the effect of scale on length and area.
- Surface area and volume of prisms and cylinders, and composite solids.
- Sampling, bias, and why a sample may not represent a population.
- Five number summary, box plots, comparing two distributions, and describing
  spread as well as centre.
- Two way tables, Venn diagrams, relative frequency and the difference between
  experimental and theoretical probability.

Some arithmetic should be completable without a calculator. Where a question
allows a calculator, say so, and state rounding expectations.

Stay away from: calculus, logarithms, formal proof by induction, and
conditional probability notation beyond a two way table reading.

READING AT YEAR 9

Passages of roughly 400 to 600 words. Include at least one text that argues a
position and one that requires reading beyond the literal, such as fiction
with an unreliable or limited narrator, or a text where tone carries the
meaning.

Ask students to evaluate the strength of an argument, identify unsupported
reasoning, explain how structure shapes a reader's response, analyse tone and
register, and compare how two texts treat a shared idea. An answer should be
defended from the text, and the model answer must name the evidence.

CONVENTIONS AT YEAR 9

- Nominalisation and its effect on formality and agency.
- Subordination, embedded clauses and sentence variety for effect.
- Register and audience, and where a shift in register is a fault.
- Punctuation of complex sentences, including where a comma changes meaning.
- Deliberate versus accidental ambiguity.
- Precision in word choice, and morphology and etymology in less familiar
  words.

WRITING AT YEAR 9

Prompts should support a sustained, controlled response. A narrative should
allow a considered structure, including a non chronological choice if the
student wants one. A persuasive prompt should support a nuanced position,
developed reasoning, evidence and a genuine counterargument. Short items may
practise argument structure, integrating and commenting on evidence, varying
sentence length for effect, and editing for precision.
