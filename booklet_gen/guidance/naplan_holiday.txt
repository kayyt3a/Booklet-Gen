NAPLAN PRACTICE HOLIDAY SUPPLEMENT

This supplement sits on top of the copyright-safe NAPLAN practice authoring
guide above, and on top of any year supplement loaded after it. Every rule in
those still applies: the originality rules, the item-writing contract, and the
domain rules for numeracy, reading, conventions of language and writing.
Nothing below relaxes any of them. What follows is what changes when the
booklet is worked in the school holidays.

THE YEAR LEVEL DOES NOT MOVE

Write at the TRUE year level requested. NAPLAN is sat in Years 3, 5, 7 and 9,
on a fixed date, against a fixed year's expectations, so a booklet that lifts
its content a year above the student is not preparing them for the test they
will actually sit. The acceleration rule used by Academic Accelerate does not
apply here and must not be borrowed.

That cuts both ways. Pitching below the year is the more common failure and it
is just as wrong: it produces a booklet a student finishes comfortably and
learns nothing from, and it tells a parent their child is ready when the test
will say otherwise. Stay at the year, cover the full range of difficulty the
year expects, and let the stretch items sit at the top of that year rather
than in the next one.

WHO IS IN THE ROOM

School is not running. There is no teacher, no classroom, no whiteboard, no
class discussion and no lesson tomorrow that fills in what today's page left
out. The only adult present is a parent, who may never have been taught this
material, may have been taught it a different way, and is not being paid to
prepare. The student may be working alone at a kitchen table.

Write for that room. A page that only makes sense to someone who was in class
last term is a page nobody in this house can rescue.

EVERYTHING THE STUDENT NEEDS IS ON THE PAGE

The base guide already requires each item to include every fact needed to
solve it. In a holiday booklet that extends from the item to the whole page.

- Define every term the first time it is used, in plain words, in the booklet
  itself. Do not assume a word was covered at school, even a word the year
  level is supposed to know.
- The mini-lesson and worked example carry the method in full, including the
  steps a teacher would normally say out loud rather than write.
- Never refer the student to a teacher, a tutor, a classroom, a school
  textbook, a previous lesson at school, a website, a video or an app. There
  is no one to ask and nothing to look up.
- Never set work that needs a partner, a group, a class discussion or an adult
  who already knows the method.
- Require nothing beyond a pencil, an eraser, paper and a ruler, and do not
  require the internet, a purchase or a calculator unless the question says
  one may be used.
- A passage is read cold, with nobody to introduce it. It must stand on its
  own from its first line, and every question about it must be answerable from
  the passage as printed.

THE ANSWER KEY IS READ BY A PARENT

In term time a wrong answer gets picked up in class. In the holidays the
answer key is the only feedback that exists, so it has to teach as well as
mark.

- Give complete working for every numeracy question, written so an adult who
  does not know the method can follow it and then explain it.
- For a reading question, the model answer must NAME the words in the passage
  that support it, so a parent can mark an answer they would not have thought
  of themselves.
- For conventions of language, say what the rule is, not only which answer is
  correct.
- For a writing task, give what a strong response contains: the shape it
  should take, the features being looked for, and what would be missing from a
  weak one. A parent cannot mark a piece of writing against a blank page.
- Where a common mistake is predictable, name it and say what the student has
  probably done.

CONTEXTS FIT A HOLIDAY, NOT A SCHOOL DAY

- Do not build questions around school routines that are not happening: roll
  call, assembly, the canteen at lunch, a lesson later that day, homework due
  Friday.
- Do not assume the family is travelling, away or spending money. A holiday
  context must work just as well for a child who is at home all week.
- Do not describe the practice as a real test, a trial test or a prediction of
  one, and do not tell a student or a parent what a result would mean for the
  test itself. That rule is in the base guide and it matters more here, where
  no teacher is present to put a score in proportion.

STARTING FROM COLD

The student has been away from the subject and may open this booklet weeks
after their last lesson. The first thing they meet must be something they can
do, and the mini-lesson must reteach the ground the practice stands on rather
than assume it survived the break.
