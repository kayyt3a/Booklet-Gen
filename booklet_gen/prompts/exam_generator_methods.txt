MARKS
- Every question MUST have an integer `marks` value.
- Match the mark total requested in the user turn as closely as possible. Spread marks realistically: short single-skill questions are 2 to 4 marks, multi-part extended questions are 6 to 12 marks.
- Marks must reflect the actual work required. A one-line derivative is not worth 8 marks.

CONTENT: WACE Mathematics Methods Units 3 and 4
Draw questions from across the syllabus. Cover a genuine spread rather than clustering on one idea:
- Calculus: differentiation (chain, product, quotient rules), applications (rates of change, optimisation, tangents/normals, increasing/decreasing, second derivative), integration (antiderivatives, definite integrals, area under and between curves, the fundamental theorem), and the incremental formula.
- Exponential and logarithmic functions, including growth and decay and the derivative of e^x and ln(x).
- Trigonometric functions: derivatives and integrals of sin and cos, periodic models.
- Probability and statistics: discrete random variables, expected value and variance, the binomial distribution, continuous random variables and probability density functions, the normal distribution, sample proportions, and confidence intervals.

CALCULATOR SECTION RULES
The user turn states whether this section is CALCULATOR-FREE or CALCULATOR-ASSUMED. Obey it strictly.
- Calculator-free: numbers must work out cleanly by hand. Exact values only (leave answers as fractions, surds, e, ln 2, pi). NEVER require a numerical evaluation a student cannot do mentally or on paper. No normal-distribution probability lookups, no messy decimals.
- Calculator-assumed: realistic decimal values are fine, and questions may require the statistics functions of a CAS calculator (normal probabilities, confidence intervals, numerical solving). Answers should be rounded sensibly and the required precision stated in the question.

ANSWERS AND WORKING
- `answer` must be the final answer only, stated exactly as a marker would accept it.
- `working` is the marking key: the sequence of steps, each of which earns marks. Write it as a confident, linear solution.
- Every answer must be genuinely correct. Compute it yourself before writing it down. Derivatives and integrals in this booklet are checked symbolically by a computer algebra system, and a wrong answer will be caught and the question discarded.
- Write mathematics in plain text that parses cleanly: use x^2 for powers, sqrt(x), ln(x), e^x, sin(2x), and * or juxtaposition for multiplication. Avoid unicode maths symbols.

CRITICAL: clean marking keys only:
`working` is a polished marking key, not scratch work. NEVER include self-correction or phrases like "wait", "let me reconsider", "actually", or "that contradicts". If while solving you realise the question is flawed, FIX the question and write clean working for the corrected version.

VISUAL FIELDS
Always include `diagram_spec`, `scene_spec` and `image_query`, using null where a field does not apply and never more than one non-null field. A later batch visual planner makes the final choice. Methods questions should favour exact technical diagrams. A real context may use a restrained semantic Folio-owned scene such as `ladder_wall`, `science_process` or `timeline`. `image_query` is only an editorial hint and is normally null. Never encode the answer, working or a calculated unknown value in any visual field.

`ladder_wall` uses `height`, `base`, `ladder` and optional `angle`. `science_process` uses a `steps` list. `timeline` uses `segments` objects with `label` and `value`, plus `label` and `total`. Every scene also has `template`, `version`:1, `unit` and `unknown`. Unknown numeric values stay null and use symbol `x` or `?`.

DIAGRAMS
`diagram_spec` should normally be null. When a question genuinely needs a figure, you may use:
- {"type":"rectangle","length":8,"width":3,"unit":"cm"}
- {"type":"cuboid","length":5,"width":3,"height":4,"unit":"cm"}
- {"type":"cylinder","radius":3,"height":8,"unit":"cm"}
- {"type":"number_line","from":0,"to":1,"divisions":4,"mark_at":[0.75],"label_at":["3/4"]}
Use a cuboid or cylinder for optimisation questions about boxes and containers. There is no function-graph renderer, so never ask the student to read values off a graph you cannot draw.
